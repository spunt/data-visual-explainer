1 The gist · for everyone

Did users assigned to Home v2 have more active app days after assignment than users assigned to control?

+0.21 days
more per user
Plausible range +0.05 days to +0.37 days

Among users kept by the saved analysis, users had 0.21 more active days than users in days 1-28 after they were assigned.

Control users averaged 6.42 active days; treatment users averaged 6.63.

≈For every 100 users in the analysis table, Home v2 adds about 21 active user-days over the 28-day follow-up window.

What kind of claim: Shows a link. Groups are compared, but nothing rules out other differences between them. Treat it as a link, not a cause.

✓ Safe to say

"In the saved analysis, treatment users averaged 0.21 more active days than control users over days 1-28 after assignment."

✗ Not this

"Home v2 caused all new users to be active 0.21 more days per month."

2 How it's built · for the curious

How the number is made

Follow one user: Alex

Alex is assigned to on 10 May 2026, sees the variant screen, is outside China, and has app activity on several later dates.

  1. 1day 0 Assigned to Home v2 and sees the variant screen
  2. 2day 0 Uses the app on assignment day; this date does not count
  3. 3day 1 Uses the app the next day counts
  4. 4day 28 Uses the app on day 28 counts
  5. 5day 29 Uses the app on day 29; outside the window

Result: Alex contributes 2 to the average. Assignment-day activity and day-29 activity do not count.

Now the general rule, one step at a time. Each step has the plain version, with the exact version one click away.

  1. Step 1 · Who is counted

    Start with users, then apply each rule

    One app user in the Home v2 experiment analysis table.

    Exactly

    One user_id from exp.assignments for experiment_id = 'home_v2', after joining to exp.exposures with saw_variant_screen = 1, excluding country_code = 'CN', and requiring at least one joined app.events activity_date in the outcome window.

    S3

  2. Step 2 · When the clock starts

    Time zero: assignment date

    Each user's clock starts on their assignment date.

    • Assignment window: The experiment admitted users assigned from 4 May through 17 May 2026. (days 0–13)
    • Days 1-28 after assignment: The 28 calendar dates after the assignment date, excluding assignment day. (days 1–28)
    Exactly

    assigned_date = CAST(a.assigned_at AS DATE).

    S3

  3. Step 3 · What counts

    Active days

    Counts how many different dates had any app activity in the outcome window.

    Exactly

    COUNT(DISTINCT act.activity_date), where activity_date is DATE(e.event_ts) from app.events grouped by user_id and UTC-derived activity_date.

    S3

  4. Step 4 · Combine

    Average across people

    Average the active-day counts within each experiment arm.

    Exactly

    AVG(active_days) grouped by arm; denominator is COUNT(*) from per_user.

    S3

  5. Step 5 · Compare

    Take the difference

    Subtract the average from the treatment average.

    mean_active_days_treatment - mean_active_days_control

    With the real numbers: 6.63 − 6.42 = 0.210

    Whose effect? No causal target; a comparison of observed numbers.

    Exactly

    diff = treatment.mean() - control.mean(); from the summary table, 6.63 - 6.42 = 0.21 days.

    S4S5

  6. Step 6 · How sure

    Show the range of plausible values

    The saved interval uses the spread of per-user active days in each arm to estimate sampling uncertainty around the treatment-control difference.

    Not covered by this range: The interval does not cover bias from post-assignment filtering, UTC-vs-local date mismatch, missing events, or whether zero-activity assigned users were dropped.

    Exactly

    se = sqrt(var_treatment / n_treatment + var_control / n_control); interval = diff +/- 1.96 * se. p comes from scipy.stats.ttest_ind(..., equal_var=False).

    S4S5

The same definition, assembled mechanically from the spec

Generated from the structured definition, not written by hand. If it disagrees with the summary above, the summary is wrong.

  1. The unit is one user. One app user in the Home v2 experiment analysis table.
  2. Keep users that meet every rule, in order: assigned to the Home v2 experiment from 4 May through 17 May 2026; saw the variant screen and was not in China; had at least one recorded activity day in days 1-28 after assignment.
  3. Time zero is the assignment date: Each user's clock starts on their assignment date.
  4. Assignment window (eligibility): days 0–13, counting the assignment date as day 0.
  5. Days 1-28 after assignment (outcome): days 1–28 after the assignment date.
  6. For each user, record active days (headline) as a count within the days 1-28 after assignment: Counts how many different dates had any app activity in the outcome window.
  7. Average the active-day counts within each experiment arm.
  8. Do this separately for each group: Control, Home v2.
  9. Subtract the control average from the treatment average.
  10. The saved interval uses the spread of per-user active days in each arm to estimate sampling uncertainty around the treatment-control difference.

3 Keep in mind · before you act on it

What could make this misleading

Complete. The latest assignment date is 17 May 2026; its days 1-28 after assignment ends 14 Jun 2026. Data are complete through 20 Jun 2026, so every window is fully observed. This number should not move as more data arrive.

Caveats

↑ May make the number look bigger than it really is↓ May make the number look smaller than it really is± The true uncertainty is larger than shown↕ Could push the number either way◎ Limits what the number can be used to conclude
◎

Not pure intent-to-treat

The readout says every assigned user is analyzed in their assigned arm, but the query keeps only users with a matching and at least one post-assignment activity day. If changes exposure or whether a user has any activity at all, the reported mean is conditional on a post-randomization outcome.

Limits what the number can be used to conclude · high importance S1 S3

↕

Zero-activity users are not in the denominator

Because per_user uses an inner join to activity, users with no recorded in days 1-28 are absent rather than counted as zero active days. This likely overstates each arm's average active days; the direction for the treatment-control difference is unknown.

Could push the number either way · high importance S3

↕

Time zone mismatch

The readout says activity is counted in each user's local time zone. The query turns event times into dates before counting active days.

Could push the number either way · medium importance S1 S3

◎

Scope is narrower than all new users

The implemented population excludes China and requires seeing the variant screen. The saved result should not be generalized to all new users without checking those exclusions.

Limits what the number can be used to conclude · medium importance S2 S3

Common misreadings

Tempting, but wrongAccurate
Home v2 caused all new users to be active 0.21 more days per month.The saved analysis shows a 0.21-day difference among exposed, non-CN users retained in the per_user table over days 1-28 after assignment.
0.21 days is a 0.21% lift.It is an absolute difference in days. Relative to the 6.42-day average, it is about a 3.3% lift.
The denominator is all assigned users.The SQL denominator is users in per_user, after the exposure join, China exclusion, and inner join to activity.
Days 1-28 are local-calendar days.The SQL derives dates with DATE(event_ts), and the source comment says event_ts is stored in UTC.

Judgment calls

What to treat as the headline

Chose: Use the 0.21-day treatment-control difference, because both readouts emphasize that number and the CSV stores the matching difference and interval.

Could have chosen: A card could instead headline the mean (6.63 days), the (+3.3%), or an rerun if zero-activity assigned users were available.

Why: The user asked for the headline number readers see, and the readout repeats +0.21 days.

What it changes: The card focuses on the difference in means and treats the relative lift as a secondary restatement.

How to state causality

Chose: Mark the saved result as associational, even though the experiment assignment was randomized.

Could have chosen: If the analysis were rerun on all assigned users as true intent-to-treat, the claim strength could be causal for the randomized population.

Why: The implemented SQL applies post-assignment exposure and activity filters, so the analyzed denominator may no longer preserve the original randomization.

What it changes: This prevents the card from repeating the readout's broader causal claim until the denominator is verified or rerun.

Where the description and the code disagree

Intent-to-treat denominator

Described

README says every assigned user is analyzed in the arm they were assigned to.

Implemented

assigned joins to exposures with saw_variant_screen = 1, then per_user inner joins to activity, excluding assigned users without a matching exposure row or without activity in days 1-28.

Impact: Narrows the denominator from all assigned users to exposed active users. The impact on the +0.21-day difference is unknown from saved outputs.

S1S3

Geographic scope

Described

The readouts describe randomized users or all new users without a country exclusion.

Implemented

The SQL filters a.country_code <> 'CN'.

Impact: Results do not cover China. Direction and size of any difference are unknown.

S2S3

Time zone for active dates

Described

README says active days use the user's local time zone.

Implemented

activity_date is DATE(e.event_ts), and the SQL comment says event_ts is stored in UTC.

Impact: Activity near local midnight can land on a different date. The effect on distinct active-day counts is unknown.

S1S3

Breadth of causal claim

Described

Readout says Home v2 causes users to be active 0.21 more days a month and applies to all new users.

Implemented

The code estimates a treatment-control difference over days 1-28 among exposed, non-CN users who had at least one activity day in the outcome window.

Impact: The implemented result is narrower than the readout claim. A true all-assigned ITT estimate could be smaller or larger.

Check yourself

Could you explain it to a colleague?

Big pictureWhat is the safest reading of the +0.21 headline?

The headline is a difference in average days among users retained in the saved analysis table, not a percent and not automatically a claim about every new user.

DetailWhich users are missing from the implemented denominator?

The per_user table inner joins assigned users to activity in the outcome window, so zero-activity users do not contribute a zero.

Apply itA user is assigned on 10 May, uses the app on 10 May, 11 May, and 8 June. Which dates count?

The implemented window excludes assignment day and includes dates through assigned_date + 28. For a 10 May assignment, 11 May is day 1 and 7 June is day 28; 8 June is outside.
